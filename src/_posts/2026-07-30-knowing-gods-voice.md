There is a moment that those who have walked with God for so many years will recognise. You are standing at a crossroads, and you genuinely do not know which way to go. It may be a job offer, a relationship, the city or country you should move to, what you should say yes or no to, and what you should quietly let go. You have prayed and fasted, asked people you trust but yet no response, and your spirit stays unsettled, and this has left you wondering whether God is actually speaking to you at all, or whether you have simply forgotten how to listen.

That feeling is not a sign that something is broken in your faith. It is actually the beginning of one of the most important journeys a believer can take: the journey into learning, truly and practically, how to recognise the voice of the God who made you.

## The Shepherd Has Already Spoken.

> *"Most assuredly, I say to you, he who does not enter the sheepfold by the door, but climbs up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber. But he who enters by the door is the shepherd of the sheep. To him the doorkeeper opens, and the sheep hear his voice; and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out. And when he brings out his own sheep, he goes before them; and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice. Yet they will by no means follow a stranger, but will flee from him, for they do not know the voice of strangers."
> John 10:1–5 (NKJV)*

What strikes me about this passage is what Jesus does not say. He does not say the sheep try to hear his voice. He does not say they occasionally catch it on a good day. He says they know it. Knowing implies something that has been cultivated over time, something that has become deeply familiar through repeated encounter. The sheep do not panic when the Shepherd speaks, they recognise Him because they have heard him before. They have spent time in his presence and therefore when a stranger calls, they do not need a theology degree to figure out something is wrong. They simply know it is not Him, and they run.

This tells you something important about how God designed this relationship. You were never meant to be perpetually confused about His voice, you were created with the capacity to know it. The question is not whether God is speaking, the question is whether you have spent enough time with Him to be familiar with how He sounds when He speaks.

## The Night Samuel Learned To Listen.

There is a young boy in the Bible who teaches us more about learning God's voice than almost anyone else. His name is Samuel, and his story is honest in a way that should bring enormous comfort to anyone who has ever confused their own thoughts with God's voice.

> *"And it came to pass at that time, while Eli was lying down in his place, and when his eyes had begun to grow so dim that he could not see, and before the lamp of God went out in the tabernacle of the Lord where the ark of God was, and while Samuel was lying down, that the Lord called Samuel. And he answered, 'Here I am!' So he ran to Eli and said, 'Here I am, for you called me.' And he said, 'I did not call; lie down again.' And he went and lay down. Then the Lord called yet again, 'Samuel!' So Samuel arose and went to Eli, and said, 'Here I am, for you called me.' He answered, 'I did not call, my son; lie down again.' (Now Samuel did not yet know the Lord, nor was the word of the Lord yet revealed to him.) And the Lord called Samuel again the third time. So he arose and went to Eli, and said, 'Here I am, for you called me.' Then Eli perceived that the Lord had called the boy. Therefore Eli said to Samuel, 'Go, lie down; and it shall be, if He calls you, that you must say, 'Speak, Lord, for Your servant hears.'' So Samuel went and lay down in his place. Now the Lord came and stood and called as at other times, 'Samuel! Samuel!' And Samuel answered, 'Speak, for Your servant hears.'"
> 1 Samuel 3:2–10 (NKJV)*

God called Samuel three times, and three times Samuel ran in the wrong direction. He heard God’s voice, real and clear enough to wake him from sleep, and he genuinely did not know it was God. This was not a moment of rebellion or spiritual failure. Samuel was serving in the temple and was in the right place. He simply had not yet learned the sound of the voice that was speaking to him.

And here is the grace in the story: God did not give up and find someone else. He called again and again and when Eli finally helped Samuel understand what was happening, and Samuel lay back down and said, "Speak, Lord, for Your servant hears," everything changed. That posture of availability, that simple and deliberate act of positioning himself to receive, was the turning point.

## How You Actually Begin To Learn God’s Voice.

Learning God's voice is not a mystical skill reserved for people who have been in church for fourty years. It is a relationship skill, and like all relationship skills, it grows through time spent together. The more you read the Word of God, the more familiar you become with the way God thinks and communicates. His written Word becomes the foundation against which everything else is tested. If something you hear contradicts what God has already said in scripture, you do not need to pray about it further because that is not His voice.

But beyond God’s Word, there is the matter of stillness. Most people are trying to hear God while moving at a pace that makes genuine listening nearly impossible. Samuel was lying down when God spoke. He was not in the middle of a busy task. He was still and in that stillness, even before he knew what he was hearing, the voice reached him. The practice of slowing down, of deliberately quieting the noise in and around you, is the basic condition under which most people start to discern the difference between their own anxious thoughts and the steady, unhurried, peace-carrying voice of God.

One of the most practical things you can do this week is to sit in silence with God for ten minutes without asking for anything. Just His presence. A posture that says: Speak, Lord, for your servant hears. You will be surprised what begins to surface in that quiet.

## His Voice Will Always Lead You Toward Life.

There is a pattern to how God speaks that becomes more recognisable over time. His voice does not produce panic. It may produce holy urgency, but it does not produce the kind of frantic, spiralling anxiety that leaves you unable to think. His voice aligns with His character, and His character is love, peace, truth, and purpose. He will not lead you somewhere that contradicts His Word. He will not speak to you in a way that tears you down or fills you with shame.

When you begin to practise this, you will make mistakes. You will sometimes move in a direction you thought was God and discover it was your own desire talking. That is part of the learning journey. Samuel got it wrong three times before he got it right. What matters is that you stay in the posture, that you keep returning to the Word of God, that you stay in community with people who know God and can help you test what you are hearing. The voice of God is not a one-time discovery but lifelong.

## You Were Made For This Conversation.

God did not design you to guess your way through your life, He designed you to hear Him. Jesus was not speaking poetically when He said His sheep know his voice. He was describing a reality that is available to you right now, in the ordinary days and the confusing seasons and the moments when everything feels uncertain.

You may be in a place today where you have been waiting for clarity and it has not come or perhaps you have heard something, but you are not sure whether to trust it. The invitation in both cases is the same. Go back to the Word, slow down, ask God to teach you the sound of His voice and then, like Samuel in the quiet of the night, position yourself to receive what God is already saying. He is not withholding Himself from you. He is drawing you into a deeper knowing.

## The Posture That Changes Everything.

Samuel's story did not end with confusion. It ended with a lifetime of prophetic clarity because he learned, in one quiet night, how to make himself available. "Speak, Lord, for your servant hears." That sentence is not complicated. It is not the product of years of theological study. It is simply the prayer of a person who has decided that God's voice matters more than their own agenda. When that becomes your posture, not just a one-time prayer but a daily orientation toward God, you will find that the voice you have been searching for has been speaking all along. You are simply learning to hear it more clearly.
