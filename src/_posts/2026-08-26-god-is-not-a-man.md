Why have you been treating God like a person who might change his mind? Perhaps, you did not do this consciously nor deliberately, but somewhere underneath all the praying and the waiting, there is an assumption that if enough time passes, He might reconsider your prayer requests and answer them. That perhaps you disappointed Him on several occasions in the past, He has also eventually changed His mind about His promises over your life. That what He said concerning your life is subject to the same conditions that govern every other relationship you have ever been in. That is a lie and you have been measuring an unchanging God against the scale of people who changed, and it has made your faith feel a lot more fragile than it was supposed to be.

## Scripture In Context.

Balaam was a prophet hired to curse Israel. Balak, the king of Moab, brought him to a high place overlooking the camp of Israel and asked him to pronounce destruction over them, but repeatedly, what came out of Balaam's mouth instead was a blessing. No matter how many different vantage points Balak moved him to, the same thing kept happening: Balaam could only say what God put in his mouth.

In the middle of one of those oracles, Balaam made a declaration that was not about Israel at all. It was about the nature of the God who was speaking through him.

> *Numbers 23:19 (NKJV) "God is not a man, that He should lie, nor a son of man, that He should repent. Has He said, and will He not do? Or has He spoken, and will He not make it good?"*

Four questions compressed into one verse. Has He said it? Will He not do it? Has He spoken it? Will He not make it good? The grammar of the verse does not leave room for ambiguity; it is presenting possibilities and the nature of God. God does not lie because He cannot. He does not repent of His Word because the conditions that cause men to change their minds do not apply to Him. He is not gathering new information, neither is He revising His position based on how things are going. He said what He said, and what He said will stand.

## The Same Truth Spoken By The Prophet Samuel.

The same truth surfaces in 1 Samuel 15, spoken by Samuel to King Saul after Saul had disobeyed the command of God and then tried to reframe his disobedience as worship.

> *1 Samuel 15:29 (NKJV) "And also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor relent. For He is not a man, that He should relent."*

The Strength of Israel does not relent. Samuel was not offering comfort in that moment; rather, he was delivering a verdict. But the same attribute that made it a verdict for Saul makes it a foundation for you. The God who does not relent on judgment is the same God who does not relent on promise. His unchanging nature does not apply selectively. What He has spoken over your life is held by the same character that held the whole counsel of heaven together before the world began.

## What This Actually Changes.

The reason this matters so practically is that most people do not struggle with believing God exists, but rather they struggle with believing He still means what He said. This is because repeated disappointment in one’s life with men and women who do not fulfill their promise brings about this unbelief. Also, watching one’s prayers go unanswered for so long that the original promise made by God starts to feel like something they imagined. So, this brings about a quiet shift in one’s faith from resting in what God said to managing expectations, and the Christian life starts to feel less like a walk with Someone trustworthy and more like a long, uncertain wait for a person who might or might not show up.

Numbers 23:19 interrupts that drift. It does not say God is unlikely to change. It says God cannot change in the way that men change, because the things that cause men to change does not exist in Him. He does not gather new information about your situation to update His position. He does not weigh up what you have done wrong and decide the promise no longer applies. He does not get tired of waiting for you to get it together and quietly revise what He told you. The promise He spoke is still exactly what it was the day He spoke it, because He is still exactly who He was the day He spoke it.

That is not a minor theological point, it is the ground you are standing on and if the ground is solid, then the question is not whether His word will hold, but whether you are willing to stop treating the wait as evidence that He changed His mind.

## What God Said About You Has Not Expired.

The word that was spoken over your life, whether through Scripture, a season of prayer or a moment when you knew you had heard from God, has not been revised. That word is not sitting in a queue waiting to see how you do before it gets released. It is not contingent on whether you have been consistent enough or faithful enough or healed enough to qualify. It was spoken by a God who knew everything about you before He said it, and He said it anyway. That same God has not changed His mind.

What changes is your proximity to the promise He made to you, disappointments may pull you away from it, fear reframes it and time makes it feel somehow distant. But none of those things reach the word itself. It is exactly where He left it, held in a nature that does not lie and does not relent, waiting for the moment He has already appointed to bring it to pass.

Stop measuring His faithfulness by your timeline because He works in His own time.
You may have carried a version of God in your mind that is more human than He actually is. A God who gets impatient. A God who updates His promises based on what you do. A God who said something once but might be persuaded to take it back. That is not the God of Numbers 23:19. The God of that verse does not operate on the logic of men, He speaks and it stands. He promises and He fulfills. Whatever He has told you, He will do it. He has spoken, and He will make it good. Therefore, whatever He has spoken over your life belongs to that category, so rest in it.
