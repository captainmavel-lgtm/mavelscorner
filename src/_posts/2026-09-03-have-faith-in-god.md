The morning after Jesus cursed the fig tree, the disciples noticed that the tree had withered from its roots overnight. Peter, remembering what Jesus had said the day before, pointed it out. Teacher, look, the fig tree which You cursed has withered away, that was the observation. Then, He turned to His disciples and gave them a command in three words: “Have faith in God.”

Jesus did not ask them to develop faith nor try to have faith or pray for more faith when you feel ready, rather, He commanded them to have it. The expectation is clear, and the command was spoken by the same Person who had just demonstrated, the night before, what faith in God actually produces when it is acted on without hesitation.

## WHAT THE WORD OF GOD SAYS.

Mark 11:22 is one of the most compressed and consequential commands in the Gospels. Jesus does not soften it. He does not preface it with a qualification about how it is difficult or that it takes time. He looks at His disciples, men who had watched Him heal the sick, feed thousands, and raise the dead, and He tells them to have faith in God as if it is the most natural and available thing in the world.

> *"Have faith in God." Mark 11:22 NKJV*

He then builds on the command immediately with one of the boldest declarations about faith in all of Scripture.

> *"For assuredly, I say to you, whoever says to this mountain, Be removed and be cast into the sea, and does not doubt in his heart, but believes that those things he says will be done, he will have whatever he says. Therefore I say to you, whatever things you ask when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you will have them." Mark 11:23-24 NKJV*

Whoever, that means not the most spiritually mature person, not the one with the longest prayer list nor one the cleanest record. Jesus places the authority of faith not in a category of person but in the nature of the belief itself. The condition is that the person speaks and does not doubt in their heart and that is the standard Jesus sets. Not the absence of all uncertainty, but the refusal to let doubt govern what the heart believes. There is a difference between a question and a doubt. A question seeks an answer. A doubt displaces the answer before it arrives. Jesus is speaking to the second one. He is saying, when you pray, do not let what you see override what you believe God has said.

## FAITH IS A COMMAND, NOT A FEELING.

The reason this distinction matters is that most people treat faith as something they wait to feel before they act on it. They say they will trust God when things start to look more hopeful, when the circumstances shift slightly, when something small moves so they know He is listening, but that is not faith.

Hebrews 11:1 defines faith with precision that Jesus's command in Mark 11 assumes.

> *"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." Hebrews 11:1 NKJV*

Substance and evidence, these are not emotional words. They are legal, structural words. The writer of Hebrews is saying that faith is not the absence of the unseen thing. It is the presence of something real that stands in place of what has not yet appeared. Faith is not pretending the mountain is not there. It is knowing that the God who commands the mountain is already greater than it, and choosing to speak from that knowledge rather than from what your eyes are currently reporting.

Every person in Hebrews 11 who is commended for their faith acted before they saw the result. Abraham left before he knew where he was going. Noah built before a single drop of rain fell. The walls of Jericho fell after seven days of walking, not before. Faith in every case preceded the visible outcome, not the other way around.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR MOUNTAIN.

There is something in your life right now that qualifies as a mountain. It may be a situation that has not moved despite how long you have prayed over it. It may be a diagnosis, a debt, a relationship that seems permanently broken, a door that has been shut so many times you have stopped knocking. Whatever it is, Jesus did not say to accept the mountain, to make peace with it, or to wait and see what happens to it. He said to speak to it.

Speaking to the mountain is not a technique. It is an act of faith that declares, before any visible change occurs, that God is greater than what stands in front of you. It is the decision to open your mouth and say what God has already said about your situation, even when everything around you is saying something different. That is what faith in God looks like in practice, not the absence of the problem but the presence of a declaration that God is bigger than it.

The battle for faith is almost never fought in the open. It is fought in the quiet of your own heart, in the space between what you believe God can do and what you are willing to declare out loud. The doubt Jesus warns against is the kind that talks you out of what you already know, that takes the size of the mountain and uses it to shrink your confidence in the God who made the ground the mountain stands on.

## GOD HAS NOT CHANGED THE TERMS. 

The command Jesus gave at the withered fig tree was not a first-century instruction that has softened with time. It is the same word over your life today, so have faith in God. Not because your situation is easy or because the outcome is already visible, but because the God who spoke those words is the same God who parted the Red Sea, shut the mouths of lions, and brought dead men out of sealed tombs. The terms of faith have not changed, neither has the God behind them changed.

Your mountain has not moved yet, but the word over it has already been spoken by Someone whose authority over every mountain is absolute. What He is waiting for is not a more impressive version of your faith. He is waiting for the version of you that opens your mouth, speaks what He has said, and refuses to let the size of the problem determine the size of your belief.

## JESUS COMMANDS US TO HAVE FAITH.

That command is not a suggestion waiting for the right conditions. It is not something Jesus said to people who had everything together. He said it standing next to a withered tree, to ordinary men who still had plenty of their own doubts and questions, and He said it as if it were completely available to them then, and it is also available to you right now.

The mountain in front of you is not bigger than the God behind the command. Have faith in God. Speak to the mountain. Do not negotiate with your doubt. Believe when you pray that you receive what you have asked for, and refuse to let what your eyes see in the meantime revise what your heart has chosen to believe.

He is not asking you to manufacture something you do not have. He is asking you to use what He has already placed in you. The faith that moves mountains is not the kind that arrives after the mountain moves. It is the kind that speaks before it does.

Jesus cursed a fig tree and it withered from its roots by morning, and He has handed that same authority to you . Do not put it down and do not wait until the circumstances feel more favourable. Pick it up today, speak to what is standing in front of you, and trust the God who has never once failed to honour the faith that refuses to quit.
