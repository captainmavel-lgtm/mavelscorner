When Mary and Martha sent word to Jesus that Lazarus was sick, the message they sent carried an urgency that is hard to miss. Lord, behold, he whom You love is sick. They used the word love. They were not making a general prayer request. They were reminding Jesus of the relationship, of how close He and Lazarus were, of what that closeness should mean in a crisis, and John's account of what happened next is one of the most quietly startling verses in the Gospels.

Jesus heard the message, He knew exactly what was happening and He stayed where He was for two more days. This was not because He did not care. 

John 11:5 states Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus not because He had missed the urgency of Lazurus situation, He stayed because He was operating on a timeline that had nothing to do with the one Mary and Martha were watching. And what He walked into four days later, when Lazarus had already been in the tomb, was something no one in that story could have prayed for, because no one had yet understood the scale of what God intended.

## WHAT THE WORD SAYS. 

Ecclesiastes 3 opens with one of the most recognised passages in Scripture. A time for everything, a season for every activity under heaven. What often gets quoted is the poetry of that list. What often gets missed is what Solomon says at the end of the reflection.

> *"He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also set eternity in the human heart; yet no one can fathom what God has done from beginning to end." Ecclesiastes 3:11 NKJV*

Beautiful in its time, not beautiful in spite of the delay, but beautiful specifically because of when it arrives. The word Solomon uses points to a completeness, a rightness, a fitness of the moment that could not have been achieved earlier. The thing that God is building in your life has a moment appointed for it, and that moment is not late. It is the exact point at which what He is doing becomes fully what He intended it to be.

The second half of that verse carries the tension honestly. He has set eternity in the human heart, yet no one can fathom what God has done from beginning to end. You were made to sense that something larger is at work. That longing in you for resolution, for the thing to finally come through, for the season to turn, is not impatience. It is eternity pressing against time inside you. But the same verse that names that longing also names its limit. You cannot see the full arc from where you stand. God can, and He is working from that vantage point, not yours.

## THE TWO DAYS THAT CHANGED EVERYTHING.

When Jesus finally told His disciples it was time to go to Lazarus, He said something that must have confused them completely. He told them Lazarus had fallen asleep and that He was going to wake him. The disciples thought He meant ordinary sleep but Jesus meant death, and then He said something that clarifies the entire two-day delay.

> *"And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there, that you may believe." John 11:15 NKJV*

Jesus was glad, because the two days He waited were precision. If He had gone immediately, Lazarus would have been healed and the story would have ended there. Something they had already seen many times before. What Jesus was moving toward was a resurrection, something that would produce a quality of faith in the people present that a healing could not have generated. The delay was not the absence of love, it was the depth of it.

Mary and Martha could not have known that while they were grieving. They could not have prayed for what Jesus was about to do, because it was outside the frame of what they thought was still possible. By the time Jesus arrived, Lazarus had been in the tomb four days. Martha said to Him, Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died. She was right about what He could have done. She had not yet understood what He was still going to do.

## WHEN GOD SEEMS TO ARRIVE LATE.

Most seasons of waiting feel exactly like the gap between the message Mary and Martha sent and the moment Jesus arrived at the tomb, you know He heard you, you know He loves you and you cannot understand why He has not moved yet. That gap is where faith is either built into something that will hold the weight of what God is about to do, or abandoned before the full picture arrives.
Isaiah 60:22 carries a promise that speaks directly into that gap.

> *"A little one shall become a thousand, and a small one a strong nation. I, the Lord, will hasten it in its time." Isaiah 60:22 NKJV*

In its time is His time. Not in your time, not in the time you calculated when you first brought the request to God, but in the time God has already set for it. And when that time comes, the same verse says He will hasten it. There is no slowness in the arrival once the appointed moment comes. What felt delayed in the waiting becomes sudden in the release. Lazarus walked out of the tomb. The stone was rolled away. People who had come to mourn left having seen something they would never stop talking about.

The delay you are living in right now is not the end of the story. It is the setting being prepared for the scale of what God has planned. You cannot see it from inside the waiting. Mary and Martha could not see it from inside their grief, but Jesus already knew exactly what He was walking toward when He stayed those two extra days.

## HIS TIMING IS NOT YOURS AND THAT IS THE POINT.

The hardest thing about trusting God's timing is that it requires you to release the version of the answer you had already planned on. Mary and Martha had planned on a healing but God had planned on a resurrection. If they had gotten what they asked for at the speed they asked for it, they would have received something smaller than what God intended. The delay was not punishment. It was preparation for abundance.

Whatever you are waiting for today, the version of it that arrives in God's time will be more complete, more established, and more fitting to the life He is building in you than the version that would have arrived on your preferred schedule. Not because delays are good in themselves, but because the God who holds your timeline sees the finished picture and He is not willing to give you a partial version of what He has already completed in His purposes.

He made everything beautiful in its time. He is making something beautiful in yours. The tomb has never had the final word.

## What Feels Like Too Late Is Exactly On Time With God.

Lazarus was four days in the tomb. By every human measurement, the moment for intervention had passed. Jesus walked in anyway and called him out by his name. That is the God you are dealing with. Not a God who is catching up to your situation but a God who has been ahead of it since before you knew it was coming.

The two days Jesus waited were not wasted. Every hour of the delay you are in right now is not wasted either. He is not slow, He is not distracted, neither has He forgotten the thing you brought to Him in prayer. He is moving toward your situation with the same calm authority He walked into Bethany with, and what He is carrying is bigger than what you asked for.

In His own time, trust in God’s timing. Not because the waiting is easy but because the God who set the timing has never once been wrong about it. He was not wrong about Lazarus and He is not wrong about you. The stone is about to be rolled away and your name is about to be called so hold on.

Mary said if You had been here. The timing that looked like absence was actually the architecture of something she could not yet see. Whatever gap you are standing in today, He is already there. He has already seen the end of it. And what He is walking toward in your life is beautiful in the time He has already appointed for it.
