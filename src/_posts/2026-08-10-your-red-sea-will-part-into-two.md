God never told you the sea would not be in your way, rather He told you it would part into two. That distinction matters more because the problem with standing at your Red Sea is that all you can see is the sea in front of you, the army right behind you, and the people around you who have started to lose their faith. You came this far following what you believed was God's direction, and now you are standing at a dead end.

That was exactly the position Israel found themselves in and exactly the position God chose to show up in.

## What The Bible Actually Records.

Exodus 14 opens with God directing Moses to turn back and camp before the sea. He tells Moses plainly that Pharaoh will pursue them, and that He will be honoured through Pharaoh and through his army. God knew what was coming before the first chariot wheel turned. The pursuit was not a crisis that caught Him off guard, it was all part of the plan.

When Pharaoh was told that Israel had fled, he changed his mind. He took six hundred choice chariots, along with all the other chariots of Egypt, with officers over every one of them, and he pursued the children of Israel. Israel lifted their eyes and saw the Egyptians marching after them, and they were very afraid. They cried out to the Lord and then turned on Moses, asking him whether there were no graves in Egypt, saying he had brought them out to die in the wilderness.

That is the human side of the moment. Real fear from real people who had genuinely followed God, now standing at the edge of a sea with an army closing behind them, wondering whether they had made a terrible mistake.

And into all of that, Moses answered the people: 

> *"Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which He will accomplish for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall see again no more forever. The Lord will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace." (Exodus 14:13-14, NKJV)*

God then told Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea and divide it. Moses stretched out his hand, and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night and made the sea into dry land and the waters were divided.

Verses 21 and 22 record it plainly: 

> *"And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea; and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea into dry land, and the waters were divided. So the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea on the dry ground, and the waters were a wall to them on their right hand and on their left." (Exodus 14:21-22, NKJV)*

Walls of water standing on their right and on their left, dry ground beneath their feet and the entire nation of Israel walked through what should have been the end of their story.

The Egyptians followed them into the midst of the sea and God looked down on the Egyptian army and troubled them. He took off their chariot wheels so that they drove with difficulty. The Egyptians said to one another that the Lord fought for Israel against them. Moses stretched his hand back over the sea and it returned to its full depth. Not one of them remained.

Verse 30 records the outcome:

> *"So the Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore." (Exodus 14:30, NKJV)*

## How This Story Applies To You.

The reason this account lands so hard is because the people standing at the sea were not unbelievers. They were people who had already seen God move. They had watched plague after plague fall on Egypt. They had walked out of four hundred years of slavery. They carried the evidence of God's power in their recent memory and they still panicked.

That is important because if you have ever reached a moment where everything you believed God said seems to have led you somewhere impossible, you are not alone in that moment. Israel stood there first and what God did for them, He recorded so that you would know what kind of God shows up when the sea is in front of you and the enemy behind you.

Fear sees water and calculates drowning but faith sees the Red Sea and remembers who created it.

Whatever is standing in front of you right now, whether it is a financial situation with no human solution, a door that has been shut so many times you have stopped knocking, a season that has stretched so long you have started to wonder whether God forgot the address, the question is not whether the obstacle is real. The question is whether the God who spoke to a sea in Exodus 14 still speaks and the answer is He still does.

## The Challenge Of Standing Still.

The hardest part of the Red Sea moment is not the sea; it is the instruction to stand still.

When everything around you is moving, when the pressure is mounting and the voices around you are getting louder and your own thoughts are telling you to do something, standing still feels like surrender or giving up dressed in spiritual language.

But "stand still" in Exodus 14 is a decision made under pressure to refuse panic when panic is the most natural response available. Israel was not asked to pretend the sea was not there. It was very much there. They were not asked to feel brave. They were terrified. They were asked to stop and let God move. That is a different thing entirely.

Standing still means refusing to let fear make the next decision. It means choosing the Word of God over the loudest voice in your mind. It means keeping your feet on the shore, because you know that the One who brought you here has not forgotten what He said. It is not the absence of fear but a decision not to be governed by it. Stand still and The Lord will fight for you.

## He Is A Miracle Working God.

Notice what Exodus 14 does not record. It does not say Moses devised a strategy at the water's edge. It does not say Israel summoned enough courage to attempt the crossing themselves. The text says God caused the sea to go back. God made the sea into dry land. God divided the waters. God troubled the Egyptian army. God took off their chariot wheels. God returned the sea to its full depth. Every miracle belongs to God.

Israel's role was to walk through what God opened. The only requirement for walking through a parted sea is that you are standing at the shore when God moves. This is what faith over fear actually means in practice; not the absence of it. 

Israel was frightened when Moses told them to stand still. Not the pretending that nothing is wrong. The sea was real, so was the army and the situation looked impossible. Faith over fear means looking at what is in front of you and choosing to believe that the God who brought you to this shore already knows what He is going to do on the other side of it.

God parted the sea anyway. Whatever is behind you and whatever is in front of you, neither of them have the final word, God does.

## You Will Not Drown At Your Red Sea.

The sea in your life right now is not a sign that God changed His mind about you. If He brought you out, He is not leaving you at the shore.

God is not intimidated by what is in front of you. He is the God who told the sea where its limits are. ***"Here your proud waves must stop,"*** He said in ***Job 38:11***. He is the God who ***"calls those things which do not exist as though they did"*** in ***Romans 4:17***. He makes a way not where things seem difficult, but where they are impossible.

Your Red Sea will part because the same God who moved it in Exodus 14 has not retired. He does not look at your impossible situation and search for an answer. He already has one.

So today, wherever you are carrying the weight of something that has no human answer, hear this: do not be afraid, stand still. The Egyptians you have been watching in your rear-view mirror for years: the fear, the doubt, the enemy that keeps announcing your failure, you shall see them again no more forever. The Lord is fighting for you right now. Hold your peace and let Him work.
